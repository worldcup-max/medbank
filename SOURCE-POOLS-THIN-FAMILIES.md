## cardio_endocrine — add these distinct concepts (cap the over-mined ones at ONE each)
Already covered (keep to ONE source each; do NOT re-mine): pheochromocytoma+β-block-after-α, thyroid storm+AF, acromegaly
cardiomyopathy, hypothyroid pericardial effusion, diabetic cardiomyopathy. Add the following NEW territory:

### Concept: Cushing's syndrome — cardiovascular consequences
Chronic cortisol excess (Cushing's) produces hypertension, a hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis (cortisol spillover onto
mineralocorticoid receptors), central obesity, glucose intolerance, and a cardiomyopathy with LV hypertrophy and diastolic
dysfunction. The cardiac risk integrates with the endocrine cause: hypertension is often resistant, hypokalaemia provokes
arrhythmia, and definitive treatment is directed at the source (pituitary adenoma, adrenal tumour, or ectopic ACTH).
Presentations: resistant hypertension with hypokalaemia and cushingoid features; choosing between medical control and
source-directed surgery; interpreting a hypokalaemic alkalosis in a hypertensive diabetic.

### Concept: Primary hyperaldosteronism — cardiac and vascular target-organ damage
Aldosterone excess independent of blood pressure drives myocardial and vascular fibrosis, LV hypertrophy, atrial
fibrillation, and stroke risk out of proportion to the hypertension. The endocrine diagnosis (raised aldosterone-to-renin
ratio, then confirmatory testing and lateralisation) changes cardiac management: an MRA (spironolactone/eplerenone) is
specifically indicated, and unilateral disease is surgically curable. Presentations: AF or LVH with hypokalaemic
hypertension; choosing the antihypertensive that also addresses the mechanism; when to pursue adrenal vein sampling.

### Concept: Hyperthyroidism — high-output failure and rate control
Thyrotoxicosis raises heart rate, contractility, and cardiac output, unmasking or causing high-output heart failure and
atrial fibrillation, with a genuine risk of thromboembolism. Management integrates both axes: β-blockade (propranolol also
blunts peripheral T4→T3) for rate and adrenergic symptoms, plus antithyroid therapy to treat the cause; a second
rate-controlling agent is never stacked on an existing β-blocker (switch, don't add). Presentations: new AF in a
thyrotoxic patient; a β-blocker choice; distinguishing high-output from low-output failure.

### Concept: Adrenal insufficiency / Addisonian crisis — cardiovascular collapse
Cortisol (and aldosterone) deficiency causes hypotension refractory to fluids and vasopressors, hyponatraemia,
hyperkalaemia, and hypoglycaemia; an Addisonian crisis presents as shock that only reverses with hydrocortisone. The
cardiovascular picture cannot be interpreted without the endocrine axis — pressors fail until glucocorticoid is replaced.
Presentations: unexplained shock with hyponatraemia/hyperkalaemia; hypotension after abrupt steroid withdrawal; the role
of stress-dose steroids peri-operatively.

### Concept: Calcium disorders and the myocardium (QT / arrhythmia)
Hypocalcaemia prolongs the QT interval (risk of torsades) and can depress contractility; hypercalcaemia shortens QT and
predisposes to bradyarrhythmia and digoxin sensitivity. The ECG change is only interpretable with the calcium and its
endocrine cause (hypoparathyroidism, vitamin-D disorder, primary hyperparathyroidism, malignancy). Presentations: a long
or short QT explained by calcium; arrhythmia after thyroidectomy (transient hypoparathyroidism); digoxin toxicity
potentiated by hypercalcaemia.

### Concept: Diabetic cardiac autonomic neuropathy — silent ischaemia
Long-standing diabetes damages cardiac autonomic nerves, producing resting tachycardia, loss of heart-rate variability,
orthostatic hypotension, and — critically — SILENT myocardial ischaemia/infarction that presents atypically (dyspnoea,
fatigue, collapse) rather than chest pain. The cardiac risk is inseparable from the endocrine duration/control.
Presentations: atypical ACS in a diabetic; unexplained resting tachycardia or orthostasis; why a diabetic's MI was missed.

### Concept: SGLT2 inhibitors / GLP-1 agonists — the diabetes-heart-failure decision
In a patient with type 2 diabetes and heart failure, drug choice is a joint cardiac-endocrine decision: SGLT2 inhibitors
reduce HF hospitalisation and CV death and are added as a distinct pillar; thiazolidinediones (pioglitazone) cause fluid
retention and are contraindicated in symptomatic HF; metformin is continued unless contraindicated. The correct move is
often to STOP an offending agent and add an SGLT2 inhibitor — but never a second agent of a class already present.
Presentations: optimising a diabetic's regimen after a HF diagnosis; recognising a drug worsening HF; the single best
add-on. (SBA discipline: ensure only ONE SGLT2 inhibitor appears among options.)

### Concept: Carcinoid heart disease — right-sided valvular involvement
Serotonin and vasoactive amines from a metastatic carcinoid tumour cause plaque deposition on right-sided heart valves,
producing tricuspid regurgitation and pulmonary stenosis (the left side is protected by pulmonary MAO). The cardiac
lesion is only explicable via the endocrine/neuroendocrine source, and urinary 5-HIAA plus somatostatin-directed therapy
link the two. Presentations: right heart failure with flushing/diarrhoea; a tricuspid murmur with an elevated 5-HIAA.

## pregnancy_cardio — add these distinct concepts (cap the over-mined ones at ONE each)
Already covered (keep to ONE source each): mitral-stenosis→AF/pulmonary-oedema, mechanical-valve anticoagulation,
peripartum cardiomyopathy. Add the following NEW territory:

### Concept: Preeclampsia / eclampsia — cardiovascular and pulmonary-oedema management
Preeclampsia is a hypertensive, multi-system disorder of pregnancy that can precipitate acute pulmonary oedema (from
raised afterload, endothelial leak, and diastolic dysfunction) and progress to eclamptic seizures. Management integrates
obstetric and cardiac priorities: control severe hypertension (labetalol, hydralazine, nifedipine), magnesium sulfate for
seizure prophylaxis, judicious diuresis for pulmonary oedema, and DELIVERY as the definitive treatment — timed against
fetal maturity and maternal stability. Presentations: flash pulmonary oedema at term; a hypertensive emergency in
pregnancy; when maternal instability mandates delivery.

### Concept: Pulmonary hypertension / Eisenmenger syndrome in pregnancy
Pulmonary arterial hypertension and Eisenmenger physiology carry very high maternal mortality; pregnancy is generally
contraindicated because the fall in systemic vascular resistance worsens right-to-left shunting and the fixed pulmonary
circulation cannot adapt to the volume load of pregnancy and delivery. Management is specialist, avoids systemic
vasodilators that worsen shunt, and centres on counselling. Presentations: cyanosis/syncope in a pregnant woman with
congenital heart disease; pre-conception counselling; peripartum right-heart decompensation.

### Concept: Aortopathy and dissection risk — Marfan / bicuspid aortic valve in pregnancy
Pregnancy's haemodynamic stress and hormonal effects on the vessel wall raise the risk of aortic dissection in Marfan
syndrome, bicuspid-valve aortopathy, and Turner syndrome, especially with a dilated aortic root. Management integrates
imaging surveillance, β-blockade to reduce shear, and a delivery/timing plan; an aortic root above threshold may
contraindicate pregnancy or mandate pre-pregnancy repair. Presentations: chest/back pain in a pregnant Marfan patient;
counselling on root diameter; mode-of-delivery decisions.

### Concept: Supraventricular tachycardia and arrhythmia in pregnancy
Pregnancy increases the frequency of SVT and other arrhythmias. Acute management mirrors the non-pregnant algorithm with
fetal-safety weighting: vagal manoeuvres then adenosine (safe) for stable SVT; synchronised cardioversion for
instability (safe in pregnancy). Rate/rhythm drug choice avoids teratogens. Presentations: palpitations with a narrow
regular tachycardia; when to cardiovert; drug selection for maintenance.

### Concept: Venous thromboembolism and PE in pregnancy
Pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state; PE is a leading cause of maternal death. Diagnosis navigates radiation concerns
(compression ultrasound first; V/Q or CTPA when needed) and treatment uses LMWH (warfarin and DOACs are avoided).
Massive PE with instability may need thrombolysis despite pregnancy. Presentations: dyspnoea/tachycardia with pleuritic
pain in pregnancy; anticoagulant choice; peripartum management of anticoagulation.

### Concept: Aortic stenosis / HOCM — fixed-output lesions in pregnancy
Fixed or dynamic left-ventricular outflow obstruction (severe aortic stenosis, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy)
tolerates pregnancy poorly because the physiological fall in afterload and rise in output are not accommodated; hypotension
and syncope result, and preload-reducing drugs are dangerous. Management preserves preload and afterload, avoids
aggressive vasodilation/diuresis, and plans delivery carefully. Presentations: exertional syncope with a systolic murmur;
why a preload-reducing agent caused collapse; delivery planning in severe AS.

### Concept: Amniotic fluid embolism and peripartum cardiovascular collapse
Amniotic fluid embolism is a rare, catastrophic peripartum event causing sudden hypoxaemia, cardiovascular collapse, and
DIC. It is a clinical diagnosis of exclusion requiring immediate resuscitation, delivery, and DIC correction. Presentations:
sudden collapse during/after labour; distinguishing it from PE, eclampsia, and haemorrhagic shock.
