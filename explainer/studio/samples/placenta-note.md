# Placenta Praevia
## Definition
- Placenta implanted partly or wholly in the lower uterine segment.
- Placenta praevia: the placenta covers the internal os. Low-lying placenta: the edge is less than 20 mm from the internal os after 16 weeks.
- Incidence is about 1 in 200 pregnancies at term.
## Risk factors
- Previous caesarean section (risk rises with each one), multiparity, maternal age 35 or more, smoking, IVF and multiple pregnancy.
## Clinical features
- Painless, bright red, recurrent vaginal bleeding after 20 weeks.
- Soft, non-tender uterus; high presenting part or malpresentation.
## Diagnosis
- Never do a digital vaginal examination until praevia has been excluded.
- Transvaginal ultrasound is safe and the most accurate test. Rescan at 32 weeks if the placenta is low at the 20-week scan, and again at 36 weeks if it persists.
## Management
- Acute bleed: ABC, two large-bore cannulae, FBC, crossmatch 4 to 6 units, CTG.
- Shocked or massive bleed: activate the major haemorrhage protocol and do a category 1 caesarean whatever the gestation.
- Stable: admit, give steroids if under 34+6 weeks and anti-D if Rh-negative.
- Planned caesarean at 36+0 to 37+0 weeks, or 34+0 to 36+6 weeks if she has bled.
## Complications
- Postpartum haemorrhage, because the lower segment contracts poorly: oxytocin, ergometrine, carboprost, tranexamic acid, then a Bakri balloon.
- Placenta accreta spectrum when the placenta lies over a caesarean scar.
