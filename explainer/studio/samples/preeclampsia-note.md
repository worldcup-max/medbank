# Pre-eclampsia
## Definition
- New hypertension (140/90 or more) after 20 weeks with proteinuria or maternal organ dysfunction.
- Severe: BP 160/110 or more, or symptoms such as headache, visual disturbance, epigastric pain.
## Pathophysiology
- Failed remodelling of the spiral arteries leads to placental ischaemia, release of anti-angiogenic factors (sFlt-1) and widespread endothelial dysfunction.
## Risk factors
- First pregnancy, previous pre-eclampsia, chronic hypertension, diabetes, renal disease, multiple pregnancy, age 40 or more, BMI 35 or more.
## Management
- Low-dose aspirin 75 to 150 mg from 12 weeks for women at high risk.
- Antihypertensives: labetalol first line, nifedipine or methyldopa as alternatives. Target BP 135/85.
- Magnesium sulphate for severe pre-eclampsia or eclampsia: 4 g IV over 5 to 15 minutes, then 1 g per hour for 24 hours.
- Delivery is the definitive treatment; timing depends on gestation and severity.
## Complications
- Eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, placental abruption, fetal growth restriction, pulmonary oedema, stroke.
