# The cardiac cycle (Guyton & Hall 15th ed, ch. 9): reference note for this plate

- One cycle at 75 bpm lasts 0.8 s; systole ~0.3 s, diastole ~0.5 s. The SA node fires (P wave), the atria contract, the AV node delays ~0.1 s, then the ventricles contract (after QRS). T wave = ventricular repolarisation, during late ejection.
- Valves are passive: each opens only when the pressure behind it exceeds the pressure ahead, and shuts when the gradient reverses.
- Atrial systole: atria contract (a wave), adding the last ~20% of ventricular filling. End-diastolic volume (EDV) ~110–130 mL.
- Isovolumetric contraction: ventricular pressure rises above atrial, AV valves close (S1); semilunar valves still shut until LV > aortic diastolic (~80 mmHg). Volume constant, pressure rises steeply (c wave in the atrium as the AV valve bulges back).
- Ejection: LV > aorta, aortic valve opens; LV and aortic pressure rise to ~120 mmHg. RV ejects into the pulmonary artery at ~25 mmHg systolic, with the same stroke volume, because pulmonary resistance is low. Stroke volume ~70 mL; end-systolic volume (ESV) ~40–60 mL; ejection fraction ~60%.
- Isovolumetric relaxation: LV falls below aorta, aortic valve closes (S2, dicrotic notch / incisura); AV valves still shut; volume constant at ESV, pressure falls. Atrial pressure rises as venous return fills the atria (v wave).
- Rapid filling: ventricular pressure falls below atrial, AV valves open; most filling happens now, passively (first third of diastole).
- Diastasis: atrial and ventricular pressures nearly equal; slow filling straight from the veins; then the next atrial systole.
- Exam points: S1 = AV valve closure (start of systole); S2 = semilunar valve closure (end of systole); the two isovolumetric phases are the only times all four valves are shut; aortic pressure stays ~80 mmHg in diastole because of elastic recoil of the aorta.
