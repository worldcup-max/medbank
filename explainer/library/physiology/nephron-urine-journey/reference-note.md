# How the nephron makes urine (lecture note)

## 1. Filtration at the glomerulus
- Blood enters the glomerulus through the afferent arteriole and leaves through the efferent arteriole.
- Net filtration pressure = glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure (~55 mmHg) − Bowman's capsule pressure (~15 mmHg) − plasma oncotic pressure (~30 mmHg) ≈ 10 mmHg.
- GFR ≈ 125 mL/min (≈180 L/day). Renal plasma flow ≈ 650 mL/min, so filtration fraction ≈ 20%.
- Constricting the efferent arteriole raises glomerular pressure and GFR; constricting the afferent arteriole lowers both.

## 2. Proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)
- Reabsorbs ~65% of filtered Na+ and water, all filtered glucose and amino acids, ~80% of bicarbonate.
- Reabsorption is iso-osmotic: tubular fluid stays ~300 mOsm/kg.

## 3. Loop of Henle — the countercurrent multiplier
- Thin descending limb: permeable to water, not to salt → water leaves, fluid concentrates to ~1200 mOsm/kg at the tip of the loop.
- Thick ascending limb: impermeable to water, actively pumps out Na+/K+/2Cl− (NKCC2) → fluid is diluted to ~100 mOsm/kg ("diluting segment").
- This builds the medullary osmotic gradient (300 → 1200 mOsm/kg). Loop diuretics (furosemide) block NKCC2.

## 4. Distal tubule and collecting duct
- DCT: Na+/Cl− reabsorption (thiazide-sensitive).
- Aldosterone: increases Na+ reabsorption and K+ secretion in the principal cells.
- ADH (vasopressin): inserts aquaporin-2 into the collecting duct → water moves out into the hypertonic medulla → concentrated urine (up to ~1200 mOsm/kg). Without ADH, dilute urine (~50–100 mOsm/kg).

## Key numbers
- Urine output ~1–2 L/day, i.e. >99% of the filtrate is reabsorbed.
