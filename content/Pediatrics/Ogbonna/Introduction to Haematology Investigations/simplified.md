# Introduction to Paediatric Haematology — the simple version

## The big picture

> **Haematology = the medicine of blood** — studying, diagnosing, treating and preventing blood diseases. Blood is one of the **largest organs** in the body.

Blood is **plasma (~60%) + cells (~40%)** — and the mix **shifts with age** (a preterm baby is up to **70% plasma**).

---

## The three cell lines

> **RBCs** (most abundant) → carry oxygen · **Platelets** (next) → clotting · **WBCs** (fewest) → defence.

A **feedback mechanism** keeps the numbers balanced.

---

## Where blood is made (haematopoiesis) — it moves with age

> **Yolk sac** (wk 2–3) → **Liver** (wk 6–30) → **Bone marrow** (from wk 20, and for life).

> When the body is stressed and needs more cells, the **liver and spleen** restart blood-making — **extramedullary haematopoiesis.**

---

## What each cell does

> **RBC** — oxygen + buffer. **Granulocytes (BEN)** — kill microbes, first line of defence. **Lymphocytes** — B cells make antibodies, T cells do cell-mediated immunity. **Platelets** — clotting.

---

## How haematology diseases are grouped

> **1. Production** — too little (anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia) or too much (polycythaemia, leucocytosis, thrombocytosis).
> **2. Abnormal haemoglobin** — sickle cell, thalassaemia.
> **3. Coagulation** — e.g. DIC.
> **4. Enzyme defects** — e.g. G6PD deficiency.

---

## How we investigate blood

> **Hb/PCV · FBC** (retic, WBC diff, platelets) **· blood film** (morphology, blasts) **· genotype · bone marrow aspirate** (cytology) **& biopsy** (histology).

---

## Putting it together

Paediatric haematology is the study of the **blood organ** — a plasma-plus-cells tissue whose composition changes with age and whose **three cell lines** (RBC, platelet, WBC) each have a job. Blood is made in **shifting sites** (yolk sac → liver → marrow), and can restart in the **liver and spleen** under stress. Diseases fall into four buckets — **production, abnormal haemoglobin, coagulation, and enzyme defects** — and are worked up with a small, logical toolkit: **Hb/PCV, FBC, blood film, genotype, and bone marrow aspirate/biopsy.**
