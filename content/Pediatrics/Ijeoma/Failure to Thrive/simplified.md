# Failure to Thrive — the simple version

## What it actually means

> **FTT = the child's weight is too low, or is FALLING across the growth chart.**

Three ways to qualify: weight **under the 3rd–5th percentile**, weight **dropping to** under that line, or weight **crossing 2 major centile lines** downward. The trend matters more than one number — a child steadily tracking the 3rd centile is fine; a child *falling* is not.

One idea unifies everything:

> **Every FTT, whatever the cause, ends in inadequate nutrition.**

---

## The three types (and the big one)

> **Organic** = a disease blocks nutrition. **Non-organic** = food/love isn't reaching the child. **Mixed** = both — and mixed is commonest.

The statistic that reframes your thinking:

> **Up to 80% of FTT is NON-organic** — neglect, poverty, feeding problems — not a hidden disease.

So a child failing to thrive with a **normal examination** is most likely non-organic. But because mixed is commonest, **look for both at once** — never stop at the first cause.

---

## Organic causes — follow the calorie

> **Can't get it in** (cleft palate, GERD, pyloric stenosis) · **can't absorb it** (coeliac, cystic fibrosis) · **can't use it** (metabolic disease) · **loses it** (diabetes, proteinuria) · **burns too much** (heart failure, hyperthyroidism, infection).

**Cystic fibrosis appears twice** — malabsorption *and* high energy needs — which is why CF kids fail so hard.

---

## Non-organic — food AND stimulation

Two ways the child ends up underfed:

> **Lack of food** — poverty, over-diluted formula (to stretch it), low milk supply from a stressed/exhausted mother.
> **Lack of stimulation** — an unstimulated child gets depressed → apathetic → **stops eating** (this is "hospitalism").

There's even a hormone route: stress raises **cortisol and adrenaline**, which **fight growth hormone**.

---

## Reading the growth chart — the golden pattern

> **Weight falls FIRST** → nutritional (often non-organic). **Weight AND height fall together** → organic growth disorder. **Head circumference falls LAST** → very severe, long-standing (brain is spared longest).

And the endocrine clue:

> **Height hit harder than weight** → suspect an endocrine/growth-hormone cause → check IGF-1 & IGFBP-3.

---

## Work-up — don't scattergun

> No single test separates organic from non-organic. **Observe the child being fed**, take a careful **diet + social history**, and limit screening to: **FBC, ESR, U&E/creatinine, urinalysis + culture, stool studies.**

Targeted extras: **sweat test** (CF clues), **T4/TSH**, **growth-hormone** work-up if height is worst.

---

## Treatment — three legs

> **Catch-up feeding (~150% of normal calories)** + **treat any underlying disease** + **long-term social support.**

Engage parents as **partners, not suspects** — blame backfires. Report genuine neglect/abuse, but most families need **support and education**, not blame. Some need foster care; return depends on **demonstrated ability to care**, not just time.

---

## Prognosis — age is everything

> After age 1, most non-organic FTT recovers to a healthy weight. **Onset before age 1 — especially under 6 months — risks lasting cognitive delay**, because that's when the brain grows fastest.

---

## Putting it together

FTT is a **falling weight**, and its final common path is always **inadequate nutrition**. Most cases are **non-organic** (neglect/poverty/feeding), many are **mixed**, so search for medical *and* social causes together. The **growth chart pattern** (weight-first = nutritional; both-together = organic; height-worst = endocrine) points the way. Treat with **catch-up calories (~150%) + disease treatment + social support**, partner with parents, and act early — because **the youngest children have the most to lose.**
