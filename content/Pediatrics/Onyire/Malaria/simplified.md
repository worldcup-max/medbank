# Malaria — the simple version

## The one-line story

> A female **Anopheles** mosquito injects **P. falciparum**, which hides in the **liver**, then bursts into the **blood** and destroys **red cells** — causing **fever + anaemia**, and, when red cells **stick inside vessels**, **severe/cerebral malaria**.

Nigeria owns a huge slice of this: **~27% of the world's cases, ~31% of the deaths.** The people who die are the **non-immune** — **under-5s, pregnant women, non-immune adults.**

---

## Life cycle in one breath

> **Mosquito → sporozoite → liver → merozoite → red blood cell → back to mosquito.**

The **liver stage (6–14 days) is silent.** Symptoms only start in the **blood stage**, when red cells rupture — that's the **fever and haemolysis.**

> The killer trick of falciparum is **cyto-adherence**: infected red cells **glue themselves to vessel walls (sequestration)** — and that is what causes the complications.

---

## Why severe malaria is deadly

> **Sequestration → blocked microvessels → hypoxia → lactic acidosis → cytokine storm.** In the brain that's **cerebral malaria**; plus **severe anaemia, hypoglycaemia, DIC.**

---

## Uncomplicated vs severe — the split that matters

> **Uncomplicated:** intermittent fever, headache, mild anaemia, mild low sugar.
> **Severe:** continuous high fever, **unconscious/seizing**, **Hb < 5**, DIC, **hypoglycaemia + lactic acidosis.**

The exam trigger: **fever + unconscious = cerebral malaria until proven otherwise.**

---

## Diagnosis — pick the right test

> **RDT** (detects HRP antigen) is fast — but **stays positive 2–4 weeks after cure**, so it can mislead.
> **Microscopy is the gold standard:** **thick film = how many** (density), **thin film = which species.**

Also check **glucose, FBC, renal function, LFTs**, and a **blood gas** if severe.

---

## Treatment — two ladders

> **Uncomplicated:** **ACT** + bring the fever down (paracetamol/diclofenac, tepid sponging). Fever should clear in **~3 days** — if not, look for another cause.
> **Severe:** **IV artesunate first-line** (until conscious), quinine second. Then fix each complication.

**Severe-malaria checklist:** airway patent · anticonvulsant for fits · **no steroids** · **transfuse if Hb < 5** · watch fluids for AKI · **check glucose 4-hourly, correct with 10% dextrose.**

> **Rule of thumb:** **Hb ≈ ⅓ × PCV.**

---

## Two special situations

> **Pregnancy:** **1st trimester = quinine + clindamycin**; **later trimesters = ACT.**
> **Prevention:** nets/insecticide + clear the bush; **chemoprophylaxis** (Fansidar in pregnancy, mefloquine, doxycycline). The big threats to control are **ACT partial resistance, RDT false negatives, and poor access.**

---

## Putting it together

Malaria is a **falciparum** infection spread by the **Anopheles** mosquito that runs **liver → blood**, destroying red cells. Most cases are **uncomplicated** (fever, mild anaemia) and clear with **ACT**. The danger is **severe malaria** — driven by **sequestration** — presenting as a child who is **unconscious, seizing, profoundly anaemic or hypoglycaemic**; that child needs **IV artesunate now**, plus airway care, anticonvulsants, transfusion if Hb < 5, and 4-hourly glucose. Because mortality stays high **even when treated**, the whole game is **speed**: recognise severe features fast and treat before the organs fail.
