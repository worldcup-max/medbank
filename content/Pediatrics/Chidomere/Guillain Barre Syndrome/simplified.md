# Guillain-Barré Syndrome — the simple version

## The one-line picture

> **A post-infectious autoimmune attack strips the myelin off peripheral nerves, causing weakness that starts in the legs and CLIMBS upward — with lost reflexes.**

Three hallmarks: **weakness + paraesthesias + hyporeflexia**, in an **ascending** pattern. The most important word is **post-infectious** — that's the whole mechanism in one word.

---

## How it happens

> An infection 1–3 weeks ago primed the immune system, which now cross-attacks **nerve myelin** ("friendly fire"). No myelin → no conduction → weakness + areflexia.

The classic trigger is **Campylobacter** (diarrhoea); others are EBV, CMV, HIV, Mycoplasma, even vaccines. **Two-thirds** recall a GI or respiratory illness a couple of weeks earlier — that lag is your biggest diagnostic clue.

Because it's **antibody-driven**, treatments that remove antibodies (**plasma exchange, IVIG**) work.

---

## The weakness — four words

> **Ascending · symmetrical · flaccid · areflexic.** Legs first, climbing to arms, trunk, breathing and face, peaking around 2 weeks.

The danger is the *direction*: as it climbs it reaches the **breathing and swallowing muscles**.

> **Up to 1 in 3 patients need a ventilator.**

Watch breathing with **NIF** (negative inspiratory force): **normal > 60 cm H₂O; heading toward 20 → get ready to ventilate.**

Also watch the **autonomics** — swinging heart rate and blood pressure can kill on their own.

---

## Diagnosis — two classics

> **CSF: albuminocytologic dissociation** — HIGH protein, NORMAL cells (protein without the white cells you'd expect in infection). May be normal very early.
> **Nerve studies: demyelination** (slowing, block, long F-waves) — but only after **2–3 weeks**, so early-normal doesn't rule it out.

Don't forget the mimics: in Nigeria, **polio** is a key differential (but polio is *asymmetric*). Others: botulism (descends), myasthenia (reflexes preserved), hypokalaemia, organophosphates.

---

## The course — reassuring but dangerous

> Usually **benign**: recovery starts in **2–3 weeks** and runs in reverse — **bulbar recovers first, legs last, reflexes last of all.**

But "usually benign" ≠ safe: **bulbar and respiratory involvement kills if treatment is delayed.**

---

## Treatment

> **Plasma exchange OR IVIG** — cut recovery time by up to **50%.**
> **Steroids alone DON'T work** (classic exam point).

Everything else is support: **ICU** if FVC < 20 mL/kg, severe bulbar palsy, or labile autonomics; **DVT prophylaxis** for paralysed limbs; autonomic drugs; and **physio/OT** to rebuild strength. Keep monitoring after recovery — some relapse.

---

## Putting it together

GBS is **ascending, symmetrical, flaccid, areflexic** weakness, **1–3 weeks after an infection** (think Campylobacter). It's an **autoimmune demyelination**, confirmed by **albuminocytologic dissociation** in CSF and **demyelinating nerve studies** (which lag 2–3 weeks). It's usually **self-limiting**, but **respiratory and autonomic failure** can kill — so monitor **NIF/FVC**, admit early to ICU, give **IVIG or plasma exchange** (not steroids alone), and support the patient through the peak.
