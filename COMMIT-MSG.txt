3D: a per-mesh ceiling, and the coccyx becomes a bone

Two changes. The first came from a measurement on a phone; the second is
the escalated item.

A CEILING, BECAUSE A BUDGET IS BLIND TO CONCENTRATION
-----------------------------------------------------
Frank opened the intercostal scene on a phone on Nigerian mobile data: 25
seconds, and 32 of 35 structures. The three it lost were the external,
internal and innermost intercostals — every muscle the scene exists to
teach. A bare rib cage.

The per-scene budget did not prevent that and could not. Two scenes can
weigh the same 21 MB and fail completely differently:

    vertebral column   48 files under 1 MB   lose one -> you lose a vertebra
    intercostals        3 files over 4 MB    lose one -> you lose the subject

A big file on a flaky link does not fail at random. It fails FIRST, and the
biggest mesh in a scene is almost always the thing the scene is about. So
concentration is a separate risk from total weight and needs its own limit.

40,000 triangles per mesh, measured against the two cases most likely to
break rather than picked:

    scapula      65,538 -> 40,000   surface moved 0.023 mm
    cerebellum  117,127 -> 40,000   surface moved 0.041 mm

The scapula is the bone RENDER-STANDARD section 5 singles out because an
exam asks a student to recognise THAT shape; the cerebellum's folia are the
finest texture in the corpus. Both come through intact. Across all 31 meshes
over the ceiling the worst mover is the internal intercostal at 0.334 mm,
and its fibre direction — the examinable fact — is still legible against the
723,154-triangle scan.

    31 meshes re-decimated from their SCANS, not from what we ship, so the
    result is what apply-mesh-budget.mjs would regenerate
    largest file in the corpus   6.08 MB -> 1.91 MB
    corpus                        317 MB -> 274 MB
    intercostal scene            21.6 MB -> ~13.4 MB

The ceiling is applied AFTER the never-go-backwards clamp and overrides it.
That is deliberate: "keeps the detail it already has" is worthless if the
file never arrives.

Not yet re-measured on a phone. One lever at a time — the per-scene budget
is untouched so the next measurement attributes cleanly.

Ruled out on the way: the slowness is not Render, which is not in the mesh
path at all (it serves IMPORT_API only; meshes come from Supabase, the app
from GitHub Pages). And it does not look like Supabase's tier either —
racing the two hosts on the same connection, GitHub Pages gave 1.52 and
4.27 Mbit/s against Supabase's 1.67 and 3.18. Same ballpark. What the
numbers show is a link that swings by 3x on the same file seconds apart and
degrades on long transfers. File size is the lever we control.

THE COCCYX
----------
BodyParts3D segments the sacrum and coccyx as ONE object, FMA16202 — a
single connected piece 145.3 mm tall where a sacrum is 100-115. The coccyx
was already on screen as real scan data but had no id, so it could not be
shown, hidden or isolated; scenes reached it only through an anchor, which
drew a marker ON TOP of the bone instead of letting a student look at it.

Cut at the minimum cross-section, z = 825.5 (147 mm^2), where the
sacrococcygeal joint would be if the scan had not fused it:

    FMA16202-sacrum   110.5 mm   188.8 cm3   (adult sacrum 100-115 mm)
    FMA16202-coccyx    34.8 mm     5.3 cm3   (adult coccyx   30-45 mm)

Both closed solids — triangles crossing the plane are split, the section is
traced into loops and capped, the caps face opposite ways. Verified by
signed volume: both positive, summing to exactly the source's 194,116.9.

FMA16202 is left alone and stops being referenced. Rewriting that id's file
would have been fifteen fewer edits, but the id names a BodyParts3D object
and a re-ingest would have silently restored the fused mesh underneath us.

THE ANCHORS HAD TO MOVE. anchor.uvw is a fraction of the PARENT's bounding
box, so trimming 34.8 mm off the sacrum moves everything riding it. The
sacrum piece keeps the fused mesh's x and y extents exactly; only z shrinks,
145.309 -> 110.499. sacral_promontory in bony-pelvis is re-derived to the
same world point, z = 903.828, uvw z 0.7786 -> 0.7089. The two coccyx
anchors are deleted — the thing they pointed at is a mesh now.

Sixteen scenes reference that mesh and all sixteen are updated. Fifteen came
from grepping what happened to be staged; the sixteenth,
gross__stomach-intestines__large-intestine, only appeared when the whole
corpus was scanned.

The validator caught a real mistake: the new coccyx structure carried a
provenance block naming the provider, and a scene must not know who renders
it — the adapter emits the CC-BY-SA credit, not the scene file. Removed.

tools/split-mesh-at-plane.mjs does the cut and is reusable. Its first
version wound both cut faces inside-out and the halves STILL summed to the
source volume, because the caps cancel either way; it now checks both
positive volumes and the sum, and records all four measured combinations in
its header so nobody re-derives it by reasoning.
