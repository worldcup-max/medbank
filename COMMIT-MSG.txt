WHAT THE RUNS ARE NOW REQUIRED TO DO
------------------------------------
A READ-BACK, in BUILD-TASK-PROMPT.md section 3 and its equivalent in
REVIEW-TASK-PROMPT.md. A run may not mark an item `built` or `done` until
it has re-staged every file it claims to have written and confirmed each
exists with an mtime later than the run started, and has reported the
read-back in `built_notes`.

This is deliberately stronger than checking the `written` and `rejected`
arrays `device_commit_files` returns, because a run that never issued the
commit at all also fails a read-back, and that cannot be ruled out as what
happened on 2026-09-11: `cranio-caudal-folding` was marked built with
1,900 words of specific proof and nothing reached the repo. The model file
has never existed, nor its render harness, nor the probe it cited, and the
scene on disk was the untouched August version. That run wrote exactly one
thing — its own queue entry — and sat `built` for nine days while six
other items were reviewed ahead of it.

A CLOCK RULE, beside it: stamp timestamps from the clock at the moment of
writing, never from a schedule. On 2026-09-22 a run wrote
`built_at: 15:05:00Z` at 14:37 — twenty-eight minutes in the future, and
exactly its next :05 cron slot. Every "built for over a day without
review" check then read that item wrong.

A FOURTH ESCALATION REASON in ESCALATIONS.md: *a run's account of itself
is contradicted by the repo*. The desk previously admitted three reasons
and an entry like cranio-caudal-folding's had no legitimate home in the
file built to catch exactly it.

A STALE-CLAIM FIX in tools/queue-set.mjs, which is the more interesting
half. BUILD-TASK-PROMPT.md has said for weeks: "if you find an item
already `building` with a `claimed_at` older than two hours, that run
died; reclaim it." The rule was right and UNREACHABLE — `--next` only ever
looked at `changes-requested` and `todo`, so no run ever *found* a
`building` item and the condition never became true. Four items sat
claimed for 8, 7, 6 and 6 days while every run stepped past them to fresh
work and reported a good day: zero `built`, four `building`, and a
reviewer with nothing to do. `--next` now surfaces a stale claim first,
with its age and the status to restore.

  A rule conditioned on noticing something is only as good as whatever
  decides what you look at. Prose cannot repair that.
